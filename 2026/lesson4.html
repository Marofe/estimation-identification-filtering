Maximum Likelihood Estimation

Note that $$ \sum_{k=1}^N\frac{1}{2}\|y_k-H_k\theta\|^2_{R_k^{-1}} = \frac{1}{2}\|y - \mathcal{H}\theta\|^2_{\mathcal{R}^{-1}} $$ where $$ y:=\begin{bmatrix} y_1\\ y_2\\ \vdots \\ y_N \end{bmatrix},\quad \mathcal{H}:=\begin{bmatrix} H_1\\ H_2\\ \vdots \\ H_N \end{bmatrix},\quad \mathcal{R}:=\text{blkdiag}(R_1,R_2,\ldots,R_N) $$

Matrix Inversion Lemma

 

Lemma: (Woodbury matrix identity) Given $A$ and $C$ non-singular matrices, then for any $U$ and $V$ of compatible dimensions, we have

 

$$ (A+UCV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1} $$

Recursive Bayesian Estimation

Given a dataset $$ Y^N:=\{y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_N\}, $$ and a prior distribution $$ \theta\sim p(\theta) $$ We want to find

$$ p(\theta|Y^N)=\frac{p(Y^N|\theta)p(\theta)}{p(Y^N)} $$

Recursive Bayesian Estimation

Given a dataset $$ Y^{N+1}:=\{y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_N,y_{N+1}\}, $$ and a prior distribution $$ \theta\sim p(\theta) $$ We want to find

$$ p(\theta|Y^{N+1})=\frac{p(Y^{N+1}|\theta)p(\theta)}{p(Y^{N+1})} $$

Recursive Bayesian Estimation

Given a dataset $$ Y^{N+1}:=\{y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_N,y_{N+1}\}, $$ and a prior distribution $$ \theta\sim p(\theta) $$ We want to find

$$ p(\theta|Y^{N+1})=\frac{p(y_{N+1},Y^{N}|\theta)p(\theta)}{p(y_{N+1},Y^{N})} $$

Recursive Bayesian Estimation

Given a dataset $$ Y^{N+1}:=\{y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_N,y_{N+1}\}, $$ and a prior distribution $$ \theta\sim p(\theta) $$ We want to find

$$ p(\theta|Y^{N+1})=\frac{p(y_{N+1}|Y^{N},\theta)p(Y^N|\theta)p(\theta)}{p(y_{N+1}|Y^{N})p(Y^N)} $$

Recursive Bayesian Estimation

Given a dataset $$ Y^{N+1}:=\{y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_N,y_{N+1}\}, $$ and a prior distribution $$ \theta\sim p(\theta) $$ We want to find

$$ p(\theta|Y^{N+1})=\frac{p(y_{N+1}|Y^{N},\theta)p(\theta|Y^N)}{p(y_{N+1}|Y^{N})} $$

Estimator Properties: bias

Definition: An estimator is said to be a unbiased if the estimate converges to the true mean value. For instance, $$ \begin{equation} \lim_{k\rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{E}\{\hat{\theta}_k|Y^k\}=\mathbb{E}[\theta] \end{equation} $$

Estimator Properties: consistency

Definition: An estimator is said to be a consistent if the estimate converges to the true value in some stochastic sense. For instance, $$ \begin{equation} \lim_{k\rightarrow \infty} \mathbb{E}\{\|\theta-\hat{\theta}_k\|_2^2|Y^k\}=0 \end{equation} $$ is the condition for consistency in the mean square sense for a non-random parameter estimator. The expectation is taken over $Y^k$.

The Cramer-Rao Low Bound

Definition: Fisher Information The Fisher Information is defined as $$ \begin{equation} J:=-\mathbb{E}\{\frac{\partial^2 \text{ln} \Lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta^2}\}|_{\theta=\theta_0}=\mathbb{E}\{[\frac{\partial \ln \Lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta}]^2\}|_{\theta=\theta_0} \end{equation} $$ where $\Lambda(\theta)=p(Y^N|\theta)$ is the likelihood function and $\theta_0$ is the true parameter value.

The Cramer-Rao Low Bound

Definition: CRLB The Cramér Rao Low Bound (CRLB) for an unbiased estimator is defined as the inverse of the respective Fisher Information. $$\begin{equation} CRLB:= J^{-1} \end{equation} $$

All unbiased estimator satisfies $$ \begin{equation} \Var\{\hat{\theta}_k\}\ge CRLB \end{equation} $$

Estimator Properties: efficiency

Definition: According to the Cramer-Rao lower bound (CRLB), the mean square error cannot be smaller than a certain quantity related to the likelihood function. If an unbiased estimator's variance is equal to the CRLB then such estimator is called Efficient. $$ \begin{equation} \Var\{\hat{\theta}_k\}=CRLB \end{equation} $$
