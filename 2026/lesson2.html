Introduction: what is a stochastic process?

A stochastic process is a probabilistic model of a system with uncertainty evolving in time.

Example:

  • Random walk;
  • Brownian Motion;
  • Geometric Brownian motion;
  • Ornstein-Uhlenbeck;
  • Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs)

Stochastic Process

Definition: Let $(\Om,\Fscr,\Prob)$ be a probability space and $T$ an index set (typically $T=\N$ for discrete time or $T=[0,\infty)$ for continuous time). A stochastic process is a collection $X=\{X_t\}_{t\in T}$ of random variables $X_t:\Om\to E$ taking values in a measurable state space $(E,\Escr)$. For fixed $\om$, the map $t\mapsto X_t(\om)$ is a sample path.

Filtration

Definition: A filtration $\{\Fscr_t\}_{t\in T}$ is an increasing family of sub-$\sigma$-algebras, $\Fscr_s\subseteq\Fscr_t\subseteq\Fscr$ for $s\le t$, representing the information available up to time $t$. The process is adapted if $X_t$ is $\Fscr_t$-measurable for every $t$; the natural choice is $\Fscr_t=\sigma(X_s:s\le t)$.

Markovian Stochastic Process

Definition: An adapted process has the Markov property if the future depends on the past only through the present: \[ \Prob(X_{k+1}\in A\mid \Fscr_k)=\Prob(X_{k+1}\in A\mid X_k) \qquad\text{a.s.} \]
"Given the present, past and future are conditionally independent".

Example: Wiener Process (Brownian Motion)

Definition: A process $\{W_t\}_{t\ge0}$ is a Standard Brownian Motion if
  • $W_0=0$ almost surely;
  • For $0\le t_0 < \dots< t_k$ the increments $W_{t_i}-W_{t_{i-1}}$ are independent;
  • $W_t-W_s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,t-s)$ for $0\le s < t$;
  • $t\mapsto W_t$ is (almost surely) continuous.

Example: Wiener Process (Brownian Motion)

Brownian motion is the continuous-time, continuous-path analogue of a random walk, introduced mathematically by Wiener and central to stochastic calculus.
Proposition: For a standard Brownian motion, $$ \E[W_t]=0\\ \Cov(W_s,W_t)=\min(s,t). $$

Example: Wiener Process (Brownian Motion)

Brownian motion is the continuous-time, continuous-path analogue of a random walk, introduced mathematically by Wiener and central to stochastic calculus.
Proposition: For a standard Brownian motion, $$ \E[W_t]=0\\ \Cov(W_0,W_t)=t \Rightarrow \text{Std} = \sqrt{t} $$

Stochastic Process In State Space

Discrete-time $$ x_{k+1}=f(x_k,u_k)+\sigma(x_k,u_k)w_k,\quad w_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q_k) $$

Assume that $w_k$ are independent of $x_k$.

For all $k=0,1,\ldots$ $$ \begin{align} \mathbb{E}[x_{k+1}]&=\mathbb{E}[f(x_k,u_k)]\\ \Cov(x_{k+1})&=\mathbb{E}[(x_{k+1}-\mathbb{E}[f(x_k,u_k)])(\bullet)^\trp] \end{align} $$
