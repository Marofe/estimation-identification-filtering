Navigation System

The navigation equations are given by
$$ \begin{aligned} \dot{C}_b^e&= C_b^e[\omega_{ib}^b]^\times-[\omega_{ie}^e]^\times C_b^e,\\ \dot{p}^e_{eb}&= v^e_{eb},\\ \dot{v}^e_{eb}&= C_b^ef_{ib}^b-2 {\omega}_{ie}^e \times {v}^e_{eb}+g^e_l \end{aligned} $$
where the position $p^e_{eb}=[x_b^e~y_b^e~z_b^e]^\trp$ is coordinated in the Earth-Centered-Earth-Fixed (ECEF) frame, and $$\omega^e_{ie}=[\omega_{e}\cos(L)~0~-\omega_{e}\sin(L)]^\trp$$ where $\omega_e$ is the Earth rotation rate.

Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)

$$ \begin{align} \tilde{\omega}_{ib}^b&= \omega_{ib}^b+ {b}_\omega+N_\omega {\varepsilon}_g, \\ \tilde{f}_{ib}^b&= f_{ib}^b+ {b}_a+N_a {\varepsilon}_a, \end{align} $$
where ${\varepsilon}^\omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and ${\varepsilon}^a \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and $b_a,b_\omega$ are the accelerometer and gyroscope biases, respectively.

Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)

The GNSS measurement model (Loosely Coupled) is given by
$$ \begin{equation} {y}= p^e_{eb}+{C}_b^e{l}^b+ \nu \end{equation} $$
where ${\nu} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, {R})$ is the GNSS noise.

GNSS: Satellite Constellation

GNSS: Least-Squares Solution

Pseudoranges from $m\geq 4$ satellites ($s_i$ known); unknowns $x=[p^\trp~~c\,\delta t]^\trp$: $$ \rho_i = \|s_i - p\| + c\,\delta t + \nu_i,\qquad i=1,\dots,m,\qquad \nu\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R) $$ Linearize at $\hat{x}$, with $u_i=(s_i-\hat{p})/\|s_i-\hat{p}\|$ the line-of-sight unit vector: $$ \Delta\rho = \rho - h(\hat{x}) \approx H\,\Delta x + \nu,\qquad H=\begin{bmatrix} -u_1^\trp & 1\\ \vdots & \vdots\\ -u_m^\trp & 1\end{bmatrix} $$ Gauss–Newton iteration (repeat until $\|\Delta\hat{x}\|$ is small):
$$ \Delta\hat{x} = (H^\trp R^{-1} H)^{-1} H^\trp R^{-1}\,\Delta\rho,\qquad \hat{x}\leftarrow\hat{x}+\Delta\hat{x} $$
Covariance: $P=(H^\trp R^{-1}H)^{-1}$; with $R=\sigma^2 I$, $\;\mathrm{GDOP}=\sqrt{\tr\,(H^\trp H)^{-1}}$.

The problem: tracking in clutter

Measurement-origin uncertainty Each scan a sensor returns many measurements:
  • at most one from the target,
  • the rest clutter / false alarms,
  • the target may be missed ($P_D < 1$).
Which measurement is the target? Unknown.

Approaches:

  • Nearest-neighbour — cheap, brittle.
  • MHT — defer decisions, costly.
  • PDA — soft decision within a scan.

Model and assumptions

Linear-Gaussian target and measurement:

$$ \begin{align} \mathbf{x}_k &= F\mathbf{x}_{k-1} + \mathbf{w}_{k-1},\quad \mathbf{w}_{k-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, Q) \\ \mathbf{y}_k &= H\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{v}_k, \quad \mathbf{v}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, R). \end{align} $$

Standing assumptions (A1) Gaussian past: $p(\mathbf{x}_k \mid Y^{k-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}, P_{k|k-1})$;
(A2) at most one measurement per target; detection prob. $P_D$;
(A3) target-originated measurement in gate with prob. $P_G$;
(A4) clutter uniform in the gate, Poisson count (density $\lambda$);
(A5) measurements conditionally independent given origin.

Validation gating

Predicted measurement and innovation covariance:

$$ \begin{align} \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{k|k-1} &= H\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}, \\ S_k &= HP_{k|k-1}H^{\trp} + R\\ \boldsymbol{\nu}_j &= \mathbf{y}_j - \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{k|k-1} \end{align} $$

Gate (Mahalanobis test):

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{G}(\gamma):=\{\boldsymbol{\nu}_j:\boldsymbol{\nu}_j^{\trp} S_k^{-1} \boldsymbol{\nu}_j \le \gamma\}\;}$$

The threshould is $\gamma$ from $\chi^2_{n_y}$ for gate probability $P_G$.
Gate volume: $$V_k = c_{n_y}\,\gamma^{n_y/2}\,|S_k|^{1/2}$$

Validation gating

PDAF: association probabilities

Events: $$ \theta_{j}=\{y_{k,j} \text{ is target-originated}\},\quad \forall j=1,\ldots,m_k\\ \theta_{0}=\{\text{no validated measurements is target-originated}\} $$

 

Event Posterior Probability:

$$\beta_{j} := \mathbb{P}(\theta_{j} \mid Y^k), \qquad \sum_{j=0}^{m_k} \beta_{j} = 1.$$

PDAF: association probabilities

Now, consider the prior event probability:

$$ \mathbb{P}(\theta_{j}\mid m_k, Y^{k-1})\propto \begin{cases} P_DP_G \mu_F(m_k-1)\frac{1}{m_k},\quad j\neq 0\\ (1-P_DP_G)\mu_F(m_k),\quad j=0 \end{cases} $$

where $$ \mu_F(\phi)=e^{-\lambda V_k }\frac{(\lambda V_k)^\phi}{\phi!} $$

 

Obs: It is assumed that the number $\phi$ of clutters inside the gate follows a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda V_k$!

PDAF: association probabilities

Thus,

$$ \beta_{j}= \frac{\mathcal{L}_j(k)}{1-P_DP_G}\beta_{0}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_j(k)}{1-P_DP_G}\frac{1-P_DP_G}{\sum_{i=1}^{m_k} \mathcal{L}_i(k)+(1-P_DP_G)}\\ \Rightarrow \beta_{j}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_j(k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{m_k} \mathcal{L}_i(k)+(1-P_DP_G)} $$ In conclusion,

$$ \beta_{j}=\begin{cases} \frac{\mathcal{L}_j(k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{m_k} \mathcal{L}_i(k)+(1-P_DP_G)},\quad j\neq 0 \\ \frac{1-P_DP_G}{\sum_{i=1}^{m_k} \mathcal{L}_i(k)+(1-P_DP_G)},\quad j=0 \end{cases} $$

Obs: $$ \beta_{j}=\mathbb{P}(\theta_{j}|Y^k) $$

PDAF: state update

We want the posterior state distribution
$$ p(x_k|Y^k)=\sum_j p(x_k|\theta_{j},Y^k)\mathbb{P}(\theta_{j}|Y^k) $$

 

So, the posteriori state distribution is a Gaussian Mixture

 

$$ \boxed{\; p(x_k|Y^k)=\sum_j \beta_{j}p(x_k|\theta_{j},Y^k)} $$

Gaussian Mixture

Moment-matching for Gaussian Mixtures For any mixture $\sum_i w_i\mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ with $\sum_i w_i=1$, the mean and covariance are $$ \mu=\sum_i w_i\mu_i,\\ \Sigma=\sum_i w_i\Bigl[\Sigma_i+(\mu_i-\mu)(\mu_i-\mu)^\trp\Bigr] $$

Thus, $$ \hat{x}_{k|k}=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\hat{x}_{k|k}^j \\ P_{k|k}=\sum_{j} \beta_{j}\Bigl[P_{k|k}^j+(\hat{x}_{k|k}^j-\hat{x}_{k|k})(\hat{x}_{k|k}^j-\hat{x}_{k|k})^\trp\Bigr] $$

Gaussian Mixture

Moment-matching for Gaussian Mixtures For any mixture $\sum_i w_i\mathcal{N}(\mu_i,\Sigma_i)$ with $\sum_i w_i=1$, the mean and covariance are $$ \mu=\sum_i w_i\mu_i,\\ \Sigma=\sum_i w_i\Bigl[\Sigma_i+(\mu_i-\mu)(\mu_i-\mu)^\trp\Bigr] $$

Thus, $$ \hat{x}_{k|k}=\hat{x}_{k|k-1}+K_k\Bigl(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}(y_{k,j}-\hat{y}_{k|k-1})\Bigr) \\ P_{k|k}=\sum_{j} \beta_{j}\Bigl[P_{k|k}^j+(\hat{x}_{k|k}^j-\hat{x}_{k|k})(\hat{x}_{k|k}^j-\hat{x}_{k|k})^\trp\Bigr] $$

PDAF: state update via the combined innovation

Define the weighted innovation

$$\boldsymbol{\nu}_k = \sum_{j=1}^{m_k} \beta_{j}\,\boldsymbol{\nu}_{k,j} $$

So, the posteriori state estimate is $$ \boxed{\;\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + K_k\,\boldsymbol{\nu}_k\;} $$ with the ordinary Kalman gain $K_k = P_{k|k-1}H^{\trp}S_k^{-1}$.

Obs: A single, probability-weighted, innovation replaces the choice of one measurement: each validated $\mathbf{y}_j$ pulls the estimate in proportion to $\beta_{j}$, how likely it is to be the target.

PDAF: covariance and the spread of the innovations

The covariance must also reflect uncertainty about which measurement was correct:

$$ \begin{align} P_{k|k} &= \underbrace{\beta_{0}P_{k|k-1}}_{\text{no T.O.}} + \underbrace{(1-\beta_{0})P_k^{c}}_{\text{correct assoc.}} + \underbrace{\tilde{P}_k}_{\text{spread}}\\ P_k^{c} &= (I - K_kH)P_{k|k-1}\\ \tilde{P}_k &= K_k\!\Big[\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m_k} \beta_{j}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{k,j}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{k,j}^{\trp} - \boldsymbol{\nu}_k\boldsymbol{\nu}_k^{\trp}\Big]K_k^{\trp} \succeq 0. \end{align} $$

 

The posterior is an exact Gaussian mixture (one component per event); the PDAF collapses it to one Gaussian by moment matching. The spread term $\tilde{P}_k$ inflates the covariance ⇒ honest uncertainty and consistency in clutter.

Only the association block distinguishes the PDAF from a plain Kalman filter.
